Mayor Johnson's ordinance will raise your rent.

The Protecting Renters Ordinance (PRO) piles new fees, red tape, and eviction restrictions onto every rental property in Chicago — including the two-flat down the street. Landlords don't absorb costs like these. Renters do.

What PRO actually does

  • Creates a citywide rental registry — landlords pay a new annual fee to register every unit.
  • Requires "just cause" for eviction or lease non-renewal, with relocation assistance owed for no-fault reasons like renovation, demolition, or condo conversion.
  • Opens a new city office to investigate and enforce landlord-tenant complaints.
  • Applies to every rental property in Chicago — a two-flat owned by a retiree, same as a 400-unit corporate building.
  • Scheduled for a City Council vote this fall; would take effect January 1, 2027.

Summarized from the Mayor's Office announcement and City Council committee coverage.

Why it matters

The math doesn't work

Someone has to pay for the new fees and red tape. It won't be the landlord.

New fees, eviction restrictions, and rehab penalties make it more expensive to own rental property — so landlords raise rents, sell their buildings, or stop renting altogether. Either way, Chicago renters end up with higher costs and fewer choices.

Generational wealth, at risk

For many Black and Latino families, a small rental building is the path to ownership.

A two-flat or three-flat is one of the few accessible ways for working families to build equity and pass property down. New costs and restrictions fall hardest on small owners who can least absorb them — pricing out the very families PRO claims to help.
